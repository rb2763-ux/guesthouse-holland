Travelling with Kids in the Dominican Republic: Dre's Honest Guide

By Dre · September 22, 2026 · 6 min read

Dre — 🌿 ECO-Tourguide #001

I've welcomed families with children at Guest House Holland for many years. I know what works and what doesn't. The Dominican Republic is genuinely good for kids — but it requires a bit more planning than a beach holiday in Europe.

The Dominican Republic is one of the most family-friendly destinations in the Caribbean, though you wouldn't always know it from the travel brochures, which tend to show couples on empty beaches. The reality is that Dominicans love children — loudly, warmly, and without reservation. A family travelling here will be welcomed everywhere.

That said, travelling with kids anywhere requires preparation, and the DR has its own specific considerations. Here is what I tell every family that contacts me before their trip.

The Beach Question

The beaches around Juan Dolio are generally calm and suitable for children. The Caribbean side of the island — where Juan Dolio sits — has gentler waves than the Atlantic north coast. For young children who are not strong swimmers, this matters a great deal.

The beach at Juan Dolio itself has a gradual entry and is shallow for a good distance out. Guayacanes, a short drive west, is similar. Both are far calmer than the beaches at Cabarete or Las Terrenas, which have surf and stronger currents.

💡 Dre's Tip: Bring your own reef-safe sunscreen from home. What's available locally is expensive and the selection is limited. Children burn quickly in Caribbean sun — apply before you leave the house, not when you arrive at the beach. And bring a rash guard for younger children; it's far more practical than reapplying sunscreen every hour.

Health and Water

This is the most important practical section for families. Children are more vulnerable to stomach upsets than adults, and the rules here are non-negotiable.

Drink only bottled water. Use it for brushing teeth. Do not give children ice in drinks unless you are at a resort or a place you fully trust. This applies even at restaurants that look clean and well-run.

Fruit and vegetables should be washed in bottled or purified water, or peeled. Fruit that you peel yourself — mangoes, bananas, papayas — is safe. Salads at local restaurants carry more risk than cooked food.

Bring a basic medical kit: children's paracetamol, oral rehydration salts, antihistamine cream for insect bites, and any prescription medication your child takes. Pharmacies exist in Juan Dolio and nearby San Pedro de Macorís, but having what you need on hand saves a stressful trip.

"The families who have the best time here are the ones who relax about everything except the water. Follow the water rule and you will almost certainly be fine." — Dre

What Kids Actually Enjoy Here

Children tend to love the Dominican Republic for reasons that have nothing to do with the resort pool. The things that genuinely delight them:

The fruit. Mangoes, papayas, passion fruit, and coconuts — all fresh, all cheap, all available at roadside stalls. Most children who have never eaten a mango straight from the tree become obsessed. A colmado stop for fresh coconut water is a highlight of many family days.

The animals. The DR has iguanas, hummingbirds, and a remarkable variety of lizards. Children who are interested in wildlife will find plenty to look at. The Parque Nacional del Este, accessible by boat from Bayahibe (about an hour from Juan Dolio), has manatees, sea turtles, and the cave paintings of the Taíno people.

The people. Dominican children are friendly and curious, and interactions between local and visiting children happen naturally at beaches and parks. Language is rarely a barrier at that age.

💡 Dre's Tip: If you're staying at Guest House Holland with children, tell me their ages and interests before you arrive. I can suggest activities that actually suit them — not just the standard tourist options. A morning looking for iguanas in the scrub behind the beach costs nothing and is often more memorable than an expensive excursion.

Practical Logistics

Car seats are not reliably available in rental cars or taxis here. If you are travelling with a young child who needs one, bring a travel car seat from home or arrange one specifically in advance. Do not assume it will be provided.

Nap times and heat are related. The hottest part of the day — roughly noon to three — is a good time to be indoors or in the shade. Plan beach mornings and late afternoons, with a rest in the middle of the day. This is how Dominicans live anyway, and it makes sense.

The DR is not a destination that requires military-level planning to enjoy with children. It is warm, welcoming, and full of things that genuinely interest young people. The basics — water, sun protection, a flexible schedule — are all you really need to get right.
