Catalina Island: Dre's Honest Take on the Day Trip Everyone Recommends

By Dre · November 17, 2026 · 6 min read

Dre — 🌿 ECO-Tourguide #001

I've sent guests to Isla Saona for years, but whenever someone asks about the quieter alternative, I point them toward Catalina. After thirty years around this coast, it's still the one I'd pick for myself if I only had one day on the water.

Catalina Island sits just off Bayahibe, a small, uninhabited strip of sand and scrub about an hour by boat from the mainland. It gets mentioned constantly by dive shops and snorkel operators along the southeast coast, usually as the "better" option compared to the bigger, busier Isla Saona - but nobody ever explains what that actually means in practice. So here's the honest version, from someone who's sent plenty of guests both places.

What Catalina Actually Is

It's small - you can walk the accessible stretch of beach in under twenty minutes - and there's no village, no souvenir stalls, and no permanent buildings beyond a few temporary lunch setups that operators bring in for the day. The appeal isn't the island itself, which is pretty but unremarkable as beaches go; it's the water around it. The reef drops off into "The Wall," a well-known dive site with a sharper drop-off than most of what you'll find along this coast, and the snorkeling just off the beach is noticeably clearer than at Juan Dolio or even Bayahibe itself.

Catalina vs. Saona - The Real Difference

Isla Saona is bigger, busier, and built around the day-trip crowd - sandbars, buffet lunches, merengue on a speaker, dozens of boats at once. It's a genuinely good day, but it's a produced one. Catalina draws smaller operators and fewer boats overall, so the beach doesn't feel as crowded and the snorkeling isn't churned up by fifty people at once. What you give up is the spectacle - no sandbar-in-the-middle-of-the-ocean photo, less entertainment built into the day. If you want the classic postcard experience, pick Saona. If you'd rather spend the day in clearer water with fewer people around you, Catalina is the better call.

💡 Dre's Tip: Book with a smaller catamaran or dive-shop operator out of Bayahibe rather than a big bus-and-boat package from La Romana - the boats are smaller, the group is smaller, and you get more actual time in the water instead of on a bus. Ask specifically how many people will be on your boat before you pay.

Getting There From Juan Dolio

This is the part most tour brochures skip. Bayahibe is roughly an hour and a half to two hours from Juan Dolio depending on traffic around La Romana, so most day-trip packages that include hotel pickup from our area start early - expect a 6:30 or 7:00am pickup. If you're staying with us for more than a few days, it's worth driving yourself to Bayahibe the evening before and starting the boat trip fresh in the morning rather than doing the full round-trip drive plus boat in a single day. It makes for a noticeably less exhausting day.

"Guests sometimes ask me which island is 'the best one.' There isn't one best island - there's the one that matches what you actually want from the day. Saona for the full show, Catalina for the quiet water. Both are real, and neither is a letdown." — Dre

Dre's Honest Take

Catalina won't give you the big group-photo moment Saona does, and if that's what you're after, don't book it expecting that. But for snorkeling, for a calmer beach, and for water you can actually see your feet in from a few meters up, it's the stronger trip. I've taken guests who did both over separate visits, and more of them come back talking about Catalina than about Saona - not because it's flashier, but because it's quieter, and quiet tends to be what people remember once they're home.
