Shopping in the Dominican Republic: What to Buy and Where

By Dre · August 4, 2026 · 6 min read

Dre — 🌿 ECO-Tourguide #001

More than thirty years in the Dominican Republic means I've seen every souvenir stall, every market, and every tourist trap the country has to offer. Here's what's actually worth your money — and what isn't.

Shopping in the Dominican Republic is one of those things that can go very well or very badly depending on where you go and what you're looking for. The tourist areas are full of the same mass-produced items you'll find across the Caribbean — plastic trinkets, generic T-shirts, and "handmade" goods that were made in a factory somewhere far away. But if you know where to look, there are genuinely good things to bring home.

What's Actually Worth Buying

The Dominican Republic produces some things that are genuinely excellent and hard to find elsewhere. These are the items I'd recommend without hesitation.

🥃 Rum
  • Brugal Añejo — the local standard; smooth, affordable, and widely available
  • Barceló Imperial — a step up; aged rum that makes a genuinely good gift
  • Bermúdez — less well-known internationally, worth trying
  • Buy at supermarkets (Nacional, La Sirena) — significantly cheaper than airport duty-free
☕ Coffee
  • Dominican coffee is excellent and underrated internationally
  • Look for Café Santo Domingo or Café Barahona — both are quality beans from the southwest mountains
  • Whole bean or ground — both travel well; buy vacuum-sealed packs
  • Available in supermarkets; much cheaper than specialty shops
🍫 Chocolate
  • The DR is one of the world's top cacao producers — the chocolate here is genuinely good
  • Öialla and Rizek are premium brands worth seeking out
  • Look for single-origin dark chocolate bars — they make excellent gifts
  • Available in better supermarkets and specialty food shops in Santo Domingo
💎 Larimar and Amber
  • Larimar — a blue volcanic stone found only in the DR; genuinely unique
  • Dominican amber — often contains prehistoric inclusions; different from Baltic amber
  • Buy from established jewellers, not beach vendors — quality varies enormously
  • In Santo Domingo's Colonial Zone, there are reputable shops with certificates

Where to Shop

The best shopping in the DR is not in the resort gift shops. Here's where to actually go.

Mercado Modelo, Santo Domingo — the main craft market in the capital. Large, busy, and full of everything from paintings to cigars to Larimar jewellery. Prices are negotiable. Go in the morning when it's less crowded and vendors are more willing to talk.

La Sirena and Nacional supermarkets — for rum, coffee, chocolate, and local food products. These are the best value for consumables. Prices are fixed and fair. You'll find branches in most towns.

Local artisan markets — smaller towns sometimes have weekend markets with genuinely handmade goods: pottery, woven baskets, carved wood. Ask locally where these happen — they're not always advertised to tourists.

"The best things I've ever bought in the DR were a bag of Barahona coffee from a supermarket and a piece of Larimar from a jeweller in the Colonial Zone who showed me the certificate. Both cost less than I expected. The worst things were the 'handmade' items from the beach vendors that fell apart within a week." — Dre

Cigars: Worth It or Not?

The Dominican Republic is one of the world's top cigar producers, and yes — Dominican cigars are genuinely excellent. If you smoke cigars or want to bring a gift for someone who does, this is a worthwhile purchase.

Buy from a proper tabaquería (cigar shop) rather than a street vendor. The quality difference is significant. In Santo Domingo, there are several reputable shops in the Colonial Zone. Brands like La Flor Dominicana, Arturo Fuente, and Davidoff (which has its factory in the DR) are all worth considering.

💡 Dre's Tip: Don't buy cigars from someone who approaches you on the street or beach. The cigars are almost always counterfeit — they look right but the tobacco inside is low quality. A real cigar shop will let you smell and examine before you buy, and the staff will know what they're selling.

What to Skip

The generic Caribbean souvenir items — the painted coconuts, the mass-produced "handmade" figurines, the T-shirts with "Dominican Republic" printed on them — are the same in every tourist destination in the region. They're cheap for a reason. If you want something that actually came from the DR and means something, spend a little more on rum, coffee, or a piece of Larimar.

Also skip the "designer" goods sold near tourist areas. Fake watches, fake handbags, fake sunglasses — they're everywhere and they're all counterfeit. Not worth the space in your bag or the risk at customs.
